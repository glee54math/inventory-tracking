# CA Common Core State Standards: Mathematics — K-8 Reference

Transcribed from *California Common Core State Standards: Mathematics*
(CDE, 2013/2014 electronic edition) for use while drafting lab plans in
`_planning/`. Boldface-in-source California additions are marked **CA**.
Star (★) = modeling standard (not applicable below grade 6). This file
covers K-8 content standards only — see the original PDF for the
Standards for Mathematical Practice (identical across all grades) and
higher-mathematics standards.

---

## Kindergarten

**Counting and Cardinality (K.CC)**
- Know number names and the count sequence: count to 100 by ones and tens
  (1); count forward from a given number (2); write numbers 0-20,
  represent a count of objects with a written numeral (3).
- Count to tell the number of objects: understand the relationship
  between numbers and quantities, connect counting to cardinality —
  one-to-one correspondence, last number = total, each successive number
  is one more (4a-c); count to answer "how many?" for up to 20 objects
  arranged various ways, or up to 10 in a scattered arrangement; count out
  a given number (5).
- Compare numbers: identify whether a group is greater than, less than,
  or equal to another group, up to 10 objects each, by matching/counting
  (6); compare two written numerals 1-10 (7).

**Operations and Algebraic Thinking (K.OA)**
- Understand addition as putting together/adding to, subtraction as
  taking apart/taking from: represent add/subtract with objects, fingers,
  drawings, sounds, acting out, expressions, or equations (1); solve
  add/subtract word problems within 10 (2); decompose numbers ≤10 into
  pairs more than one way (3); for any number 1-9, find the number that
  makes 10 (4); fluently add and subtract within 5 (5).

**Number and Operations in Base Ten (K.NBT)**
- Work with numbers 11-19 to gain foundations for place value: compose/
  decompose 11-19 into ten ones + some further ones (1).

**Measurement and Data (K.MD)**
- Describe and compare measurable attributes: describe measurable
  attributes (length, weight) of one or more objects (1); directly
  compare two objects with a shared attribute, describe the difference
  (2).
- Classify objects and count the number in each category: classify into
  given categories, count, sort by count — category counts ≤10 (3).

**Geometry (K.G)**
- Identify and describe shapes (squares, circles, triangles, rectangles,
  hexagons, cubes, cones, cylinders, spheres): describe objects using
  shape names and positional terms (above/below/beside/in front
  of/behind/next to) (1); name shapes regardless of orientation/size (2);
  identify as 2D ("flat") or 3D ("solid") (3).
- Analyze, compare, create, and compose shapes: analyze/compare 2D and 3D
  shapes using informal language (sides, vertices/corners, equal side
  length) (4); model shapes from components (sticks, clay balls),
  drawing (5); compose simple shapes to form larger shapes (6).

## Grade 1

**Operations and Algebraic Thinking (1.OA)**
- Represent and solve problems involving addition and subtraction: add/
  subtract within 20 to solve word problems (add-to, take-from, put-
  together, take-apart, compare), unknown in all positions (1); add three
  whole numbers whose sum ≤20 (2).
- Understand and apply properties of operations and the relationship
  between addition and subtraction: apply commutative/associative
  properties (need not name them) (3); understand subtraction as an
  unknown-addend problem (4).
- Add and subtract within 20: relate counting to addition/subtraction
  (5); add/subtract within 20, fluent within 10, using counting-on,
  making-ten, decomposing, relating add/sub, creating equivalent known
  sums (6).
- Work with addition and subtraction equations: understand meaning of
  the equal sign, determine true/false equations (7); determine the
  unknown whole number in an equation relating three whole numbers (8).

**Number and Operations in Base Ten (1.NBT)**
- Extend the counting sequence: count to 120 starting at any number (1).
- Understand place value: two digits of a two-digit number represent
  tens and ones, incl. special cases of 10, 11-19, and the decade numbers
  (2a-c); compare two two-digit numbers using >, =, < (3).
- Use place value understanding and properties of operations to add and
  subtract: add within 100 (2-digit + 1-digit, 2-digit + multiple of 10)
  (4); mentally find 10 more/less than a number (5); subtract multiples
  of 10 in range 10-90 (6).

**Measurement and Data (1.MD)**
- Measure lengths indirectly and by iterating length units: order three
  objects by length, compare indirectly via a third object (1); express
  length as a whole number of same-size length units, no gaps/overlaps
  (2).
- Tell and write time: hours and half-hours, analog and digital (3).
- Represent and interpret data: organize/represent/interpret data with
  up to 3 categories (4).

**Geometry (1.G)**
- Reason with shapes and their attributes: distinguish defining vs.
  non-defining attributes, build/draw shapes with defining attributes
  (1); compose 2D shapes (rectangles, squares, trapezoids, triangles,
  half-/quarter-circles) or 3D shapes to create composites (2); partition
  circles/rectangles into two and four equal shares, describe using
  halves/fourths/quarters, understand more shares = smaller shares (3).

## Grade 2

**Operations and Algebraic Thinking (2.OA)**
- Represent and solve problems involving addition and subtraction:
  add/subtract within 100 to solve one- and two-step word problems (1).
- Add and subtract within 20: fluently add/subtract within 20 using
  mental strategies; know all sums of two one-digit numbers from memory
  by end of grade (2).
- Work with equal groups of objects to gain foundations for
  multiplication: determine odd/even for a group ≤20, write an equation
  expressing an even number as sum of two equal addends (3); use addition
  to find the total for rectangular arrays up to 5×5, write as a sum of
  equal addends (4).

**Number and Operations in Base Ten (2.NBT)**
- Understand place value: three digits of a 3-digit number represent
  hundreds/tens/ones, incl. special cases (1a-b); count within 1000,
  skip-count by 2s/5s/10s/100s **CA** (2); read/write numbers to 1000
  in base-ten numerals, number names, expanded form (3); compare two
  3-digit numbers using >, =, < (4).
- Use place value understanding and properties of operations to add and
  subtract: fluently add/subtract within 100 (5); add up to four
  two-digit numbers (6); add/subtract within 1000, concrete models or
  drawings (7); use estimation strategies for reasonable estimates **CA**
  (7.1); mentally add/subtract 10 or 100 to/from a number 100-900 (8);
  explain why strategies work, using place value and properties (9).

**Measurement and Data (2.MD)**
- Measure and estimate lengths in standard units: measure length with
  appropriate tools (1); measure twice with different-length units,
  describe the relationship (2); estimate lengths in inches, feet,
  centimeters, meters (3); measure to find how much longer one object is
  (4).
- Relate addition and subtraction to length: solve word problems
  involving lengths in the same units (5); represent whole numbers on a
  number line, represent sums/differences within 100 on it (6).
- Work with time and money: tell/write time to the nearest 5 minutes
  with a.m./p.m., know time relationships (minutes in an hour, etc.)
  **CA** (7); solve word problems with dollar bills/quarters/dimes/
  nickels/pennies using $ and ¢ (8).
- Represent and interpret data: generate measurement data, show on a
  line plot (9); draw a picture graph and bar graph (single-unit scale)
  for up to 4 categories, solve put-together/take-apart/compare problems
  from a bar graph (10).

**Geometry (2.G)**
- Reason with shapes and their attributes: recognize/draw shapes with
  specified attributes (number of angles, equal faces), identify
  triangles/quadrilaterals/pentagons/hexagons/cubes (1); partition a
  rectangle into rows/columns of same-size squares, count them (2);
  partition circles/rectangles into two, three, or four equal shares,
  describe using halves/thirds/half-of/third-of, recognize equal shares
  of identical wholes need not be the same shape (3).

## Grade 3

**Operations and Algebraic Thinking (3.OA)**
- Represent and solve problems involving multiplication and division:
  interpret products of whole numbers (equal groups) (1); interpret
  whole-number quotients (equal shares or equal groups) (2); use
  multiplication/division within 100 to solve word problems (equal
  groups, arrays, measurement) (3); determine the unknown in a
  multiplication/division equation relating three whole numbers (4).
- Understand properties of multiplication and the relationship between
  multiplication and division: apply commutative/associative/
  distributive properties (need not name formally) (5); understand
  division as an unknown-factor problem (6).
- Multiply and divide within 100: fluently multiply/divide within 100
  using the relationship between the two or properties; know all
  products of two one-digit numbers from memory by end of grade (7).
- Solve problems involving the four operations, and identify and
  explain patterns in arithmetic: solve two-step word problems with all
  four operations, represent with an equation using a letter for the
  unknown, assess reasonableness via estimation/rounding (8); identify
  arithmetic patterns (addition/multiplication table), explain using
  properties (9).

**Number and Operations in Base Ten (3.NBT)**
- Use place value understanding and properties of operations to perform
  multi-digit arithmetic (a range of algorithms may be used): round
  whole numbers to nearest 10 or 100 (1); fluently add/subtract within
  1000 (2); multiply one-digit whole numbers by multiples of 10 in range
  10-90 (3).

**Number and Operations — Fractions (3.NF)** (limited to denominators
2, 3, 4, 6, 8)
- Develop understanding of fractions as numbers: understand 1/b as the
  quantity from partitioning a whole into b equal parts, a/b as a parts
  of size 1/b (1); understand a fraction as a point on a number line —
  represent 1/b (2a) and a/b (2b) on a number line diagram; explain
  equivalence and compare fractions by reasoning about size: same
  size/point = equivalent (3a); recognize/generate simple equivalent
  fractions, explain why (3b); express whole numbers as fractions,
  recognize fractions equivalent to whole numbers (3c); compare two
  fractions with the same numerator OR denominator by reasoning, record
  with >,=,< and justify, only valid for same whole (3d).

**Measurement and Data (3.MD)**
- Solve problems involving measurement and estimation of intervals of
  time, liquid volumes, and masses of objects: tell/write time to the
  nearest minute, solve add/subtract word problems with time intervals
  (1); measure/estimate liquid volume and mass in grams, kilograms,
  liters; solve one-step word problems (add/sub/mult/div) with masses or
  volumes in the same units (2).
- Represent and interpret data: draw a scaled picture graph and scaled
  bar graph for several categories, solve one-/two-step "how many
  more/less" problems from scaled bar graphs (3); generate measurement
  data by measuring to the nearest 1/4 inch, show on a line plot with
  whole/half/quarter units (4).
- Geometric measurement — understand concepts of area and relate area to
  multiplication and addition: recognize area as an attribute of plane
  figures, understand area measurement via unit squares (5a-b); measure
  area by counting unit squares (6); relate area to multiplication and
  addition: find area by tiling = multiplying side lengths (7a); multiply
  side lengths to find areas in real-world/math problems (7b); use tiling
  to show the distributive property (a×(b+c)=a×b+a×c) concretely (7c);
  recognize area as additive, find areas of rectilinear figures by
  decomposing (7d).
- Geometric measurement — recognize perimeter as an attribute of plane
  figures and distinguish between linear and area measures: solve
  real-world/math problems involving perimeter, find unknown side
  lengths, same perimeter/different area or vice versa (8).

**Geometry (3.G)**
- Reason with shapes and their attributes: understand shared attributes
  across categories (e.g. rhombuses/rectangles share "4 sides") define a
  larger category (quadrilaterals); recognize rhombuses/rectangles/
  squares as quadrilaterals, draw quadrilaterals outside those
  subcategories (1); partition shapes into parts with equal areas,
  express each part's area as a unit fraction of the whole (2).

## Grade 4

**Operations and Algebraic Thinking (4.OA)**
- Use the four operations with whole numbers to solve problems: interpret
  a multiplication equation as a comparison (35 = 5×7 as "5 times as many
  as 7"), represent verbal multiplicative comparisons as equations (1);
  multiply/divide to solve word problems involving multiplicative
  comparison, distinguish from additive comparison (2); solve multistep
  word problems with whole numbers/whole-number answers using the four
  operations, incl. problems with remainders to interpret, represent with
  an equation using a letter, assess reasonableness via mental
  computation/estimation/rounding (3).
- Gain familiarity with factors and multiples: find all factor pairs for
  a number 1-100, recognize a number is a multiple of each of its
  factors, determine if a number 1-100 is a multiple of a given one-digit
  number, determine if a number 1-100 is prime or composite (4).
- Generate and analyze patterns: generate a number or shape pattern from
  a given rule, identify features not explicit in the rule itself (5).

**Number and Operations in Base Ten (4.NBT)** (expectations limited to
whole numbers ≤1,000,000)
- Generalize place value understanding for multi-digit whole numbers:
  recognize a digit in one place = 10× the digit to its right (1);
  read/write multi-digit numbers in base-ten numerals, number names,
  expanded form, compare using >,=,< (2); round multi-digit whole numbers
  to any place (3).
- Use place value understanding and properties of operations to perform
  multi-digit arithmetic: fluently add/subtract multi-digit numbers using
  the standard algorithm (4); multiply a whole number of up to 4 digits
  by a 1-digit number, and multiply two 2-digit numbers, using place
  value/properties, illustrate with equations/arrays/area models (5);
  find whole-number quotients/remainders with up to 4-digit dividends and
  1-digit divisors, using place value/properties/relationship to
  multiplication, illustrate with equations/arrays/area models (6).

**Number and Operations — Fractions (4.NF)** (limited to denominators
2,3,4,5,6,8,10,12,100)
- Extend understanding of fraction equivalence and ordering: explain why
  a/b = (n×a)/(n×b) using visual models, use to generate equivalent
  fractions (1); compare two fractions with different numerators/
  denominators (common denominator/numerator, or benchmark like 1/2),
  record with >,=,<, valid only for same whole (2).
- Build fractions from unit fractions by applying and extending previous
  understandings of operations on whole numbers: understand a/b (a>1) as
  a sum of 1/b's (3a); understand add/subtract of fractions as joining/
  separating parts of the same whole (3a); decompose a fraction into a
  sum of fractions with the same denominator multiple ways, justify with
  a visual model (3b); add/subtract mixed numbers with like denominators
  (3c); solve word problems involving add/subtract of fractions with like
  denominators and same whole (3d); understand a fraction a/b as a
  multiple of 1/b (4a); understand a multiple of a/b as a multiple of
  1/b, multiply a fraction by a whole number (4b); solve word problems
  involving multiplying a fraction by a whole number (4c).
- Understand decimal notation for fractions, and compare decimal
  fractions: express a fraction with denominator 10 as an equivalent
  fraction with denominator 100, add two fractions with respective
  denominators 10 and 100 (5); use decimal notation for fractions with
  denominator 10 or 100 (6); compare two decimals to hundredths by
  reasoning about size, valid only for same whole, record with >,=,<,
  justify using a number line or other visual model **CA** (7).

**Measurement and Data (4.MD)**
- Solve problems involving measurement and conversion of measurements
  from a larger unit to a smaller unit: know relative sizes of
  measurement units within one system (km/m/cm, kg/g, lb/oz, l/ml,
  hr/min/sec), express a larger unit in terms of a smaller unit, record
  in a two-column table (1); use the four operations to solve word
  problems involving distances, time intervals, liquid volumes, masses,
  money, incl. simple fractions/decimals and unit conversion, represent
  with diagrams incl. number line diagrams with a measurement scale (2);
  apply area/perimeter formulas for rectangles in real-world/math
  problems (3).
- Represent and interpret data: make a line plot to display data in
  fractions of a unit (1/2, 1/4, 1/8), solve add/subtract-of-fractions
  problems using line plot data (4).
- Geometric measurement — understand concepts of angle and measure
  angles: recognize angles as formed by two rays sharing an endpoint,
  understand angle measurement via fraction of a circular arc, a
  "one-degree angle" = 1/360 of a circle (5a-b); measure angles in
  whole-number degrees with a protractor, sketch angles of a given
  measure (6); recognize angle measure as additive, solve add/subtract
  problems for unknown angles using an equation (7).

**Geometry (4.G)**
- Draw and identify lines and angles, and classify shapes by properties
  of their lines and angles: draw points/lines/line segments/rays/angles
  (right/acute/obtuse)/perpendicular/parallel lines, identify in 2D
  figures (1); classify 2D figures by presence/absence of parallel or
  perpendicular lines or angles of a specified size, recognize/identify
  right triangles, incl. special triangles (equilateral/isosceles/
  scalene) and special quadrilaterals (rhombus/square/rectangle/
  parallelogram/trapezoid) **CA** (2); recognize a line of symmetry,
  identify line-symmetric figures, draw lines of symmetry (3).

## Grade 5

**Operations and Algebraic Thinking (5.OA)**
- Write and interpret numerical expressions: use parentheses/brackets/
  braces, evaluate expressions with them (1); write simple expressions
  that record a calculation, interpret without evaluating (2); express a
  whole number 2-50 as a product of its prime factors **CA** (2.1).
- Analyze patterns and relationships: generate two numerical patterns
  using two given rules, identify relationships between corresponding
  terms, form/graph ordered pairs on a coordinate plane (3).

**Number and Operations in Base Ten (5.NBT)**
- Understand the place value system: recognize a digit = 10× the digit
  to its right and 1/10 of the digit to its left (1); explain patterns
  in zeros/decimal placement when multiplying/dividing by powers of 10,
  use whole-number exponents for powers of 10 (2); read/write/compare
  decimals to thousandths in base-ten numerals, number names, expanded
  form (3a-b); round decimals to any place (4).
- Perform operations with multi-digit whole numbers and with decimals
  to hundredths: fluently multiply multi-digit whole numbers using the
  standard algorithm (5); find whole-number quotients with up to 4-digit
  dividends and 2-digit divisors, using place value/properties/
  relationship of operations (6); add/subtract/multiply/divide decimals
  to hundredths using concrete models or drawings and place-value
  strategies (7).

**Number and Operations — Fractions (5.NF)**
- Use equivalent fractions as a strategy to add and subtract fractions:
  add/subtract fractions with unlike denominators incl. mixed numbers,
  using equivalent fractions (1); solve word problems involving
  add/subtract of fractions with unlike denominators for the same whole,
  use benchmark fractions/number sense to assess reasonableness (2).
- Apply and extend previous understandings of multiplication and
  division to multiply and divide fractions: interpret a fraction as
  division of numerator by denominator, solve word problems involving
  division of whole numbers with fraction/mixed-number answers (3);
  interpret the product (a/b)×q as a parts of a partition of q into b
  equal parts, find area of a rectangle with fractional side lengths by
  tiling with unit squares of the appropriate fraction side length (4a-
  b); interpret multiplication as scaling — compare product size to one
  factor based on the other factor (5a); explain why ×(fraction>1)
  increases and ×(fraction<1) decreases the result, relate to
  a/b=(n×a)/(n×b) (5b); solve real-world problems involving
  multiplication of fractions and mixed numbers (6); apply and extend
  understanding of division to divide unit fractions by whole numbers
  and whole numbers by unit fractions: interpret/compute division of a
  unit fraction by a non-zero whole number (7a); interpret/compute
  division of a whole number by a unit fraction (7b); solve real-world
  problems involving division of unit fractions by whole numbers and
  whole numbers by unit fractions (7c).

**Measurement and Data (5.MD)**
- Convert like measurement units within a given measurement system:
  convert among different-sized standard measurement units within one
  system, use in solving multi-step real-world problems (1).
- Represent and interpret data: make a line plot to display data in
  fractions of a unit (1/2,1/4,1/8), use fraction operations to solve
  problems from line-plot data (2).
- Geometric measurement — understand concepts of volume and relate
  volume to multiplication and to addition: recognize volume as an
  attribute of solid figures, understand volume measurement via unit
  cubes (3a-b); measure volume by counting unit cubes (4); relate volume
  to multiplication/addition: find volume of a right rectangular prism
  with whole-number side lengths by packing with unit cubes, show it
  equals multiplying edge lengths (5a); apply V=l×w×h and V=b×h formulas
  for right rectangular prisms with whole-number edge lengths (5b);
  recognize volume as additive, find volumes of composite solid figures
  (5c).

**Geometry (5.G)**
- Graph points on the coordinate plane to solve real-world and
  mathematical problems: use a pair of perpendicular number lines
  (axes) to define a coordinate system, understand x-/y-coordinate
  correspondence to the axes (1); represent real-world/math problems by
  graphing points in the first quadrant, interpret coordinate values in
  context (2).
- Classify two-dimensional figures into categories based on their
  properties: understand that attributes of a category of 2D figures
  also belong to all subcategories (3); classify 2D figures in a
  hierarchy based on properties (4).

## Grade 6

**Ratios and Proportional Relationships (6.RP)**
- Understand ratio concepts and use ratio reasoning to solve problems:
  understand the concept of a ratio, use ratio language (1); understand
  unit rate a/b for ratio a:b (b≠0), use rate language (2); use ratio and
  rate reasoning to solve real-world/math problems — tables of
  equivalent ratios/tape diagrams/double number lines/equations: make
  tables of equivalent ratios, find missing values, plot on a coordinate
  plane, compare ratios using tables (3a); solve unit rate problems incl.
  unit pricing and constant speed (3b); find a percent of a quantity as a
  rate per 100, solve for the whole given a part and the percent (3c);
  use ratio reasoning to convert measurement units (3d).

**The Number System (6.NS)**
- Apply and extend previous understandings of multiplication and
  division to divide fractions by fractions: interpret/compute quotients
  of fractions, solve word problems involving division of fractions by
  fractions (1).
- Compute fluently with multi-digit numbers and find common factors and
  multiples: fluently divide multi-digit numbers using the standard
  algorithm (2); fluently add/subtract/multiply/divide multi-digit
  decimals using the standard algorithm for each (3); find the GCF of two
  numbers ≤100 and LCM of two numbers ≤12, use the distributive property
  to express a sum with a common factor as a multiple of a sum with no
  common factor (4).
- Apply and extend previous understandings of numbers to the system of
  rational numbers: understand positive/negative numbers describe
  opposite directions/values, use in real-world contexts, explain the
  meaning of 0 (5); understand a rational number as a point on the
  number line, extend number lines/coordinate axes to negative
  coordinates — opposite signs = opposite sides of 0, opposite of the
  opposite = the number itself (6a); signs in ordered pairs indicate
  quadrant location, reflections across axes (6b); find/position
  integers and rational numbers on a line or plane (6c); understand
  ordering and absolute value of rational numbers — interpret
  inequalities as relative number-line position (7a); write/interpret/
  explain order statements in real-world contexts (7b); understand
  absolute value as distance from 0, interpret as magnitude in a
  real-world situation (7c); distinguish absolute-value comparisons from
  order statements (7d); solve real-world/math problems by graphing
  points in all four quadrants, use coordinates/absolute value to find
  distances with a shared coordinate (8).

**Expressions and Equations (6.EE)**
- Apply and extend previous understandings of arithmetic to algebraic
  expressions: write/evaluate numerical expressions with whole-number
  exponents (1); write expressions that record operations with numbers
  and letters (2a); identify parts of an expression using math terms
  (sum/term/product/factor/quotient/coefficient), view parts as a single
  entity (2b); evaluate expressions at specific variable values, incl.
  from real-world formulas, follow order of operations (2c); apply
  properties of operations to generate equivalent expressions (3);
  identify when two expressions are equivalent regardless of the value
  substituted (4).
- Reason about and solve one-variable equations and inequalities:
  understand solving an equation/inequality as finding which values from
  a set make it true, use substitution to check (5); use variables to
  represent an unknown or any number in a specified set (6); solve
  real-world/math problems by writing/solving equations of form x+p=q
  and px=q for nonnegative rational p, q, x (7); write an inequality of
  form x>c or x<c for a real-world/math constraint, recognize infinitely
  many solutions, represent on a number line (8).
- Represent and analyze quantitative relationships between dependent
  and independent variables: use variables to represent two related
  quantities, write an equation expressing one as a function of the
  other, analyze the relationship via graphs/tables and relate to the
  equation (9).

**Geometry (6.G)**
- Solve real-world and mathematical problems involving area, surface
  area, and volume: find area of right triangles/other triangles/special
  quadrilaterals/polygons by composing/decomposing (1); find volume of a
  right rectangular prism with fractional edge lengths by packing with
  unit cubes of the appropriate fraction edge length, apply V=lwh and
  V=bh (2); draw polygons in the coordinate plane given vertex
  coordinates, find side lengths sharing a coordinate (3); represent 3D
  figures using nets of rectangles/triangles, use to find surface area
  (4).

**Statistics and Probability (6.SP)**
- Develop understanding of statistical variability: recognize a
  statistical question as one anticipating variability in the data (1);
  understand a data set's distribution can be described by center,
  spread, and overall shape (2); recognize a measure of center
  summarizes with a single number, a measure of variation describes
  spread with a single number (3).
- Summarize and describe distributions: display numerical data on a
  number line — dot plots, histograms, box plots (4); summarize numerical
  data sets in context: report number of observations (5a); describe
  the attribute measured, incl. units (5b); give quantitative
  center (median/mean) and variability (interquartile range/mean
  absolute deviation) measures, describe pattern/deviations in context
  (5c); relate choice of center/variability measure to the data's shape
  and context (5d).

## Grade 7

**Ratios and Proportional Relationships (7.RP)**
- Analyze proportional relationships and use them to solve real-world
  and mathematical problems: compute unit rates with ratios of fractions,
  incl. lengths/areas/other units (1); recognize and represent
  proportional relationships: test for equivalent ratios in a table or a
  straight line through the origin on a graph (2a); identify the
  constant of proportionality (unit rate) in tables/graphs/equations/
  diagrams/verbal descriptions (2b); represent proportional
  relationships by equations (2c); explain what a point (x,y) means in
  context, esp. (0,0) and (1,r) (2d); use proportional relationships to
  solve multistep ratio and percent problems — simple interest, tax,
  markups/markdowns, gratuities/commissions, fees, percent increase/
  decrease, percent error (3).

**The Number System (7.NS)**
- Apply and extend previous understandings of operations with fractions
  to add, subtract, multiply, and divide rational numbers: describe
  situations where opposite quantities combine to 0 (1a); understand
  p+q as a distance |q| from p in the sign-determined direction, show a
  number and its opposite sum to 0, interpret sums in real-world
  contexts (1b); understand subtraction as adding the additive inverse,
  show distance between two rationals = absolute value of their
  difference (1c); apply properties of operations to add/subtract
  rational numbers (1d); understand multiplication extends to rational
  numbers via the distributive property, incl. (-1)(-1)=1 and sign
  rules, interpret products in context (2a); understand integers can be
  divided (nonzero divisor), every quotient of integers is rational,
  sign-placement equivalences, interpret quotients in context (2b);
  apply properties to multiply/divide rational numbers (2c); convert a
  rational number to a decimal via long division, know it terminates or
  repeats (2d); solve real-world/math problems with the four operations
  on rational numbers (3).

**Expressions and Equations (7.EE)**
- Use properties of operations to generate equivalent expressions:
  add/subtract/factor/expand linear expressions with rational
  coefficients (1); understand rewriting an expression can reveal how
  quantities in a problem relate (2).
- Solve real-life and mathematical problems using numerical and
  algebraic expressions and equations: solve multistep real-life/math
  problems with positive/negative rationals in any form, convert forms as
  needed, assess reasonableness via mental computation/estimation (3);
  use variables to construct equations/inequalities from real-world/math
  problems: solve word problems leading to px+q=r and p(x+q)=r forms,
  compare algebraic vs. arithmetic solution approaches (4a); solve word
  problems leading to inequalities of form px+q>r or px+q<r, graph the
  solution set and interpret in context (4b).

**Geometry (7.G)**
- Draw, construct, and describe geometrical figures and describe the
  relationships between them: solve problems involving scale drawings —
  actual lengths/areas from a scale drawing, reproduce at a different
  scale (1); draw geometric shapes with given conditions (freehand,
  ruler/protractor, technology), focus on triangle construction from
  three angle/side measures — unique/multiple/no triangle (2); describe
  2D cross-sections of 3D figures (right rectangular prisms and
  pyramids) (3).
- Solve real-life and mathematical problems involving angle measure,
  area, surface area, and volume: know and use circle area/circumference
  formulas, give an informal derivation of their relationship (4); use
  supplementary/complementary/vertical/adjacent angle facts in a
  multi-step problem to write/solve equations for an unknown angle (5);
  solve real-world/math problems involving area/volume/surface area of
  2D/3D objects composed of triangles/quadrilaterals/polygons/cubes/
  right prisms (6).

**Statistics and Probability (7.SP)**
- Use random sampling to draw inferences about a population: understand
  statistics gain population information from a sample, generalizations
  are valid only for representative samples, random sampling tends to
  produce representative samples (1); use data from a random sample to
  draw inferences about a population, generate multiple samples to gauge
  variation, estimate how far off an estimate might be (2).
- Draw informal comparative inferences about two populations: informally
  assess visual overlap of two numerical distributions with similar
  variability, express center difference as a multiple of a variability
  measure (3); use center/variability measures from random samples to
  draw informal comparative inferences about two populations (4).
- Investigate chance processes and develop, use, and evaluate
  probability models: understand probability as a number 0-1 expressing
  likelihood, larger = more likely (5); approximate a chance event's
  probability via long-run relative frequency, predict relative
  frequency given a probability (6); develop a probability model and use
  it to find probabilities of events, compare to observed frequencies and
  explain discrepancies: develop a uniform model by assigning equal
  probability to all outcomes (7a); develop a model (possibly non-
  uniform) by observing frequencies from a chance process (7b); find
  probabilities of compound events using organized lists/tables/tree
  diagrams/simulation: understand compound-event probability as fraction
  of matching outcomes in the sample space (8a); represent sample spaces
  for compound events using organized lists/tables/tree diagrams,
  identify outcomes matching an everyday-language event description (8b);
  design/use a simulation to generate frequencies for compound events
  (8c).

## Grade 8

**The Number System (8.NS)**
- Know that there are numbers that are not rational, and approximate
  them by rational numbers: know numbers that aren't rational are
  irrational, understand every number has a decimal expansion — rational
  numbers' expansions eventually repeat, convert a repeating decimal
  expansion to a rational number (1); use rational approximations of
  irrationals to compare their size, locate them on a number line, and
  estimate expressions like π² (2).

**Expressions and Equations (8.EE)**
- Work with radicals and integer exponents: know and apply properties of
  integer exponents to generate equivalent numerical expressions (1); use
  square/cube root symbols to represent solutions to x²=p and x³=p (p
  positive rational), evaluate square roots of small perfect squares and
  cube roots of small perfect cubes, know √2 is irrational (2); use
  single-digit × integer-power-of-10 form to estimate very large/small
  quantities, express how many times as much one is as another (3);
  perform operations on numbers in scientific notation, incl. mixed
  decimal/scientific notation, choose appropriate measurement units,
  interpret scientific notation from technology (4).
- Understand the connections between proportional relationships, lines,
  and linear equations: graph proportional relationships, interpret the
  unit rate as slope, compare two proportional relationships represented
  differently (5); use similar triangles to explain why slope m is the
  same between any two points on a non-vertical line, derive y=mx and
  y=mx+b (6).
- Analyze and solve linear equations and pairs of simultaneous linear
  equations: solve linear equations in one variable — give examples with
  one/infinite/no solutions by transforming to x=a, a=a, or a=b form
  (7a); solve linear equations with rational coefficients incl. the
  distributive property and collecting like terms (7b); analyze/solve
  pairs of simultaneous linear equations: understand solutions correspond
  to graph intersection points (8a); solve systems of two linear
  equations in two variables algebraically and by graphing/estimating,
  solve simple cases by inspection (8b); solve real-world/math problems
  leading to two-variable linear equation systems (8c).

**Functions (8.F)**
- Define, evaluate, and compare functions: understand a function as a
  rule assigning exactly one output per input, the graph = the set of
  (input, output) ordered pairs (function notation not required) (1);
  compare properties of two functions represented differently
  (algebraically/graphically/numerically/verbally) (2); interpret y=mx+b
  as a linear function with a straight-line graph, give examples of
  non-linear functions (3).
- Use functions to model relationships between quantities: construct a
  function modeling a linear relationship between two quantities,
  determine rate of change and initial value from a description or two
  (x,y) values incl. from a table/graph, interpret rate of change/initial
  value in context (4); describe qualitatively the functional
  relationship between two quantities from a graph (increasing/
  decreasing, linear/nonlinear), sketch a graph from a verbal description
  (5).

**Geometry (8.G)**
- Understand congruence and similarity using physical models,
  transparencies, or geometry software: verify properties of rotations/
  reflections/translations experimentally — lines to lines of same
  length (1a), angles to angles of same measure (1b), parallel lines to
  parallel lines (1c); understand 2D-figure congruence as a rotation/
  reflection/translation sequence, describe such a sequence for congruent
  figures (2); describe the effect of dilations/translations/rotations/
  reflections on 2D figures using coordinates (3); understand 2D-figure
  similarity as a rotation/reflection/translation/dilation sequence,
  describe such a sequence for similar figures (4); use informal
  arguments to establish facts about triangle angle sum and exterior
  angle, angles formed by a transversal cutting parallel lines, and the
  AA similarity criterion (5).
- Understand and apply the Pythagorean Theorem: explain a proof of the
  Pythagorean Theorem and its converse (6); apply it to find unknown
  side lengths of right triangles in real-world/math problems in 2D and
  3D (7); apply it to find the distance between two points in a
  coordinate system (8).
- Solve real-world and mathematical problems involving volume of
  cylinders, cones, and spheres: know and use the volume formulas for
  cones, cylinders, and spheres (9).

**Statistics and Probability (8.SP)**
- Investigate patterns of association in bivariate data: construct/
  interpret scatter plots for bivariate measurement data, describe
  patterns — clustering, outliers, positive/negative/linear/nonlinear
  association (1); know lines are widely used to model relationships
  between two quantitative variables, informally fit a line to a linear
  scatter plot and assess fit by closeness of points (2); use a linear
  model's equation to solve problems in context, interpreting slope and
  intercept (3); understand patterns of association also appear in
  bivariate categorical data via two-way frequency/relative-frequency
  tables, construct/interpret such a table and use relative frequencies
  to describe possible association (4).

## Glossary notes relevant to bucket design

From the CCSS Glossary (Tables 1 and 2), the "problem situation types"
below are the canonical scenario classes CCSSM itself identifies for
addition/subtraction and multiplication/division — useful as a starting
point for bucket lists in relevant labs, since CCSSM's own authors
consider "unknown in a different position" to be a materially different
(and harder) scenario class, not just a cosmetic variant:

**Addition/subtraction situation types** (Table 1): Add To (result
unknown / change unknown / start unknown), Take From (same three),
Put Together/Take Apart (total unknown / addend unknown / both addends
unknown), Compare (difference unknown / bigger unknown / smaller
unknown, each with "more" and "fewer" phrasing variants).

**Multiplication/division situation types** (Table 2): Equal Groups,
Arrays/Area, and Compare — each crossed with Unknown Product / Group
Size Unknown ("how many in each group?" division) / Number of Groups
Unknown ("how many groups?" division).
