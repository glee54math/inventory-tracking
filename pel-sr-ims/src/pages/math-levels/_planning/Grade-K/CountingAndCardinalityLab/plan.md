# CountingAndCardinalityLab

**Proposed slot:** MK1 — provisional, confirm at build time per project convention.
**Grade:** K
**Standards covered:** K.CC.1 (count to 100 by ones and tens; count forward from a given number), K.CC.2 (count forward from a given number, within 100), K.CC.3 (write numbers 0-20, represent a count of objects with a written numeral), K.CC.4a-c (cardinality — one-to-one correspondence, last number named = total, each successive number is one more), K.CC.5 (count to answer "how many?" for up to 20 objects arranged various ways or up to 10 scattered; count out a given number), K.CC.6 (compare two groups up to 10 objects each — greater than/less than/equal to), K.CC.7 (compare two written numerals 1-10).
**Relationship to existing labs:** No existing coverage — first lab for this domain and the first lab in the Grade-K folder.
**Audience framing:** Built for a student seeing this content for the first time, regardless of the standard's posted grade level. This is literally a Kindergartner's first formal math content, so Explore sections must use concrete, draggable/clickable objects (dots, counters, simple pictures) at every step — no bare numerals standing in for quantities until the numeral-writing/comparing units. Short sentences, read-aloud friendly text, no assumed vocabulary (e.g. define "more than" and "fewer" the first time they appear).

## Units

### Unit 1: Counting On and Counting Forward
- **Goal:** You will be able to count out loud in order, starting from 1 or from any number someone gives you.
- **Standard:** K.CC.1, K.CC.2
- **Buckets:**
  - Count by ones starting at 1 (short run, e.g. to 20)
  - Count by ones starting at 1 (long run, up to 100)
  - Count by tens (10, 20, 30... to 100)
  - Count forward starting from a given non-1 number (e.g. "start at 14 and keep going") — including starting mid-decade (e.g. start at 27, cross into the 30s) since the decade-boundary transition (29→30, 39→40) is the genuine difficulty, not plain sequencing
- **Requires intro (CFU gate):** Yes — this is the first unit in the lab and introduces the count sequence itself; common misconception is dropping or garbling decade transitions (e.g. saying "...28, 29, 20-10..." instead of "...29, 30...").
- **Memorization fact (periodic re-check candidate):** Yes — the count sequence (1-100, including decade words) is exactly the kind of long-term memorized fact the reintroEverySolved pattern exists for; a student who once sequenced correctly can still forget a decade transition weeks later.

### Unit 2: Writing and Matching Numerals
- **Goal:** You will be able to write the numeral for a number you hear or count, and match a count of objects to its numeral, from 0 to 20.
- **Standard:** K.CC.3
- **Buckets:**
  - Write the numeral for a spoken/heard number name (0-20)
  - Write the numeral to match a shown picture-count of objects (0-20)
  - Identify/select the correct numeral among visually similar look-alikes (e.g. distinguishing a shown "6" from "9", or "1" from "7") — a true "which numeral is this" bucket distinct from production
  - Numerals that are commonly reversed or confused in early writing (6/9, 2/5) presented as a recognition check, not just a free-write
- **Requires intro (CFU gate):** Yes — numeral writing is a new symbolic skill; common misconception is confusing visually similar numerals (6/9, 2/5) or writing digits in mirror/reversed form, which is developmentally typical at this age and worth a direct check rather than assuming it away.
- **Memorization fact (periodic re-check candidate):** Yes — numeral shapes (0-20) are a fixed visual fact worth periodic re-checking, since early reversals (6/9) can silently resurface after a long gap even once a student passes the initial check.

### Unit 3: How Many? (Cardinality and Counting Objects)
- **Goal:** You will be able to count a group of objects and know that the last number you say tells you how many there are in total.
- **Standard:** K.CC.4a-c, K.CC.5
- **Buckets:**
  - Counting objects arranged in a line or array (visually easiest — one-to-one correspondence is spatially obvious)
  - Counting objects arranged in a scattered/random layout, up to 10 (the genuinely hard case the standard calls out by name — nothing lines objects up for you, so double-counting or skipping is easy)
  - Counting objects arranged in a circle (a layout where it's easy to lose track of the starting object and recount it)
  - Counting up to 20 objects in a varied/mixed arrangement (extends the line/array case to the larger range K.CC.5 specifies)
  - Counting OUT a given number of objects from a larger pile (e.g. "give me 7 counters" from a pile of 15) — the reverse-direction skill, genuinely different from counting a fixed group that's already there
- **Requires intro (CFU gate):** Yes — cardinality itself (the idea that the LAST number counted is the total, not just a label on the last object touched) is a core misconception point; many first-time counters recount or believe each new count restarts rather than accumulates.
- **Memorization fact (periodic re-check candidate):** No — this is a reasoning/procedure skill (apply one-to-one correspondence correctly), not a fact to memorize; a student who understands it can always re-derive correct counting.

### Unit 4: Comparing Groups and Numerals
- **Goal:** You will be able to tell whether one group has more, fewer, or the same amount as another group, and tell which written number is bigger.
- **Standard:** K.CC.6, K.CC.7
- **Buckets:**
  - Compare two groups of objects by matching one-to-one (lining them up to see which runs out first) — up to 10 each
  - Compare two groups of objects by counting each group and comparing the counts — up to 10 each
  - Groups with a close/small difference (e.g. 6 vs 7) vs. groups with an obvious/large difference (e.g. 3 vs 9) — close comparisons are the genuinely harder bucket
  - Equal groups (same count, different arrangement/spacing) — the trap bucket for a student who judges "more" by how much space a group takes up rather than by count
  - Compare two written numerals 1-10 directly (no objects shown) — a distinct, more abstract skill than comparing physical groups
- **Requires intro (CFU gate):** Yes — the "takes up more space = more" misconception (ignoring actual count in favor of visual spread/density) is extremely common at this age and must be directly targeted, not assumed away.
- **Memorization fact (periodic re-check candidate):** No — comparison is a reasoning skill applied fresh to whatever numbers/groups appear; nothing here is a fixed fact to memorize.

## Open questions / flags for the builder
- K.CC.4c ("each successive number name refers to a quantity that is one larger") could arguably be its own small check-for-understanding question inside Unit 3 rather than a separate unit — flagging that the builder may want to fold a "show me one more than this group" sub-task into Unit 3's CFU rather than Unit 1's, since it's as much about cardinality as sequencing.
- Counting to 100 by ones (K.CC.1) is a long, repetitive skill for Explore/Practice to render well visually — the builder should decide whether Practice samples shorter sub-ranges (e.g. "continue counting from 47 to 60") rather than requiring a full 1-100 recitation in one problem, since a single problem spanning the full range may not fit the engine's per-problem step model well.
- Unit 2 and Unit 3 both touch "numeral ↔ count" matching from slightly different angles (producing a numeral vs. counting objects); the builder should keep Unit 2's focus strictly on the written-numeral skill and Unit 3's focus strictly on the counting-process skill to avoid overlapping bucket coverage.
