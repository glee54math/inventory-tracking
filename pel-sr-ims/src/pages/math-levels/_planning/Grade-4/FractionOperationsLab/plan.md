# Fraction Operations Lab

**Proposed slot:** MG9 — provisional, confirm at build time per project convention.
**Grade:** 4
**Standards covered:** 4.NF.3b (decompose a fraction into a sum of fractions with the same denominator, multiple ways, justify with a visual model), 4.NF.3c (add/subtract mixed numbers with like denominators), 4.NF.3d (solve word problems involving addition/subtraction of fractions with like denominators and the same whole), 4.NF.4a (understand a/b as a multiple of 1/b), 4.NF.4b (understand a multiple of a/b as a multiple of 1/b; multiply a fraction by a whole number), 4.NF.4c (solve word problems involving multiplication of a fraction by a whole number), 4.MD.4 (make a line plot displaying data in fractions of a unit — 1/2, 1/4, 1/8 — and solve addition/subtraction-of-fractions problems using data from the line plot).
**Relationship to existing labs:** This is a direct SEQUEL to the already-built `MG9_IntroToFractionsLab`. Assume the student has already completed that lab's four units (Fraction Forms, Equivalent Fractions, Comparing Fractions, Simplifying Fractions) and can represent a fraction in proper/improper/mixed form, build equivalent fractions, compare fractions, and simplify to lowest terms, all fluently. This lab does NOT re-teach any of that — its job is the OPERATIONS on fractions (decomposing, adding, subtracting, multiplying by a whole number) that `IntroToFractionsLab` deliberately left out. Denominators throughout Units 1-4 are drawn from the same general range `IntroToFractionsLab`'s own units already use (small denominators, roughly 2-10 — see `FractionForms.tsx`'s `properFraction()`/`improperFraction()` helpers and `ComparingFractions.tsx`'s `sameDenomPair()`, both drawing from that range) for visual/continuity consistency across the two labs. Unit 5 (the 4.MD.4 line-plot capstone) is the one exception — see its entry below and the open question about denominator set.
**Audience framing:** Built for a student seeing this content for the first time, regardless of the standard's posted grade level. Every new operation here (decomposing, adding/subtracting including mixed-number regrouping, multiplying by a whole number) needs a visual model (fraction bars, consistent with `IntroToFractionsLab`'s own diagram set) introduced before any pure symbol manipulation — this is especially true for the mixed-number subtraction-with-borrowing case, which is the single hardest visual-to-symbolic jump in this lab.

## Units

### Unit 1: Decomposing Fractions
- **Goal:** See a fraction as a sum of same-size unit pieces, and break it into a sum of smaller same-denominator fractions more than one way.
- **Standard:** 4.NF.3a/3b
- **Buckets:** (a) decompose into unit fractions only (e.g., 5/6 = 1/6 + 1/6 + 1/6 + 1/6 + 1/6); (b) decompose a fraction into exactly two non-unit parts, with more than one valid split shown or requested (e.g., 5/6 = 2/6 + 3/6, also = 1/6 + 4/6) — the "multiple ways" requirement from the standard text, so a single correct split can't be the whole task; (c) decompose a fraction whose numerator is close to or exceeds its denominator (e.g., 7/6), testing that the decomposition idea still works past one whole, not just for proper fractions.
- **Requires intro (CFU gate):** Yes — decomposing a fraction into a SUM is a new idea not covered by anything in `IntroToFractionsLab` (which covered forms/equivalence/comparison/simplifying but never addition-as-a-concept); CFU should include a generalization check — "can 5/6 be split into unit fractions in only one way, but into two bigger pieces in more than one way — true or false, and show a second way."
- **Memorization fact (periodic re-check candidate):** No.

### Unit 2: Adding and Subtracting Fractions and Mixed Numbers (Like Denominators)
- **Goal:** Add and subtract fractions and mixed numbers that share a denominator, using the "same-size pieces, just count them" idea from Unit 1.
- **Standard:** 4.NF.3b/3c
- **Buckets:** (a) add two proper fractions, like denominator, sum stays proper (no regrouping); (b) add two proper fractions, like denominator, sum is improper and must be converted to a mixed number — the classic forgotten final step; (c) subtract two proper fractions, like denominator, no borrowing needed; (d) add or subtract two mixed numbers, like denominator, where no regrouping of the whole-number parts is needed; (e) subtract two mixed numbers, like denominator, where the fractional part of the first number is SMALLER than the fractional part being subtracted, forcing the student to "unbundle" one whole into den/den before subtracting (e.g., 3 1/4 − 1 3/4) — the single hardest, most load-bearing edge case in this unit, and it must be reachable by the generator, not just theoretically possible.
- **Requires intro (CFU gate):** Yes — the borrow-a-whole mixed-number subtraction case (bucket e) is a well-documented 4th grade stumbling block; CFU must walk through unbundling one whole into den/den pieces with an explicit visual (fraction bar), not just state the rule.
- **Memorization fact (periodic re-check candidate):** No.

### Unit 3: Fraction Word Problems — Add and Subtract
- **Goal:** Solve real-world word problems that require adding or subtracting fractions or mixed numbers with the same denominator.
- **Standard:** 4.NF.3d
- **Buckets:** (a) a straightforward "combine two amounts" addition word problem; (b) a "how much more / how much is left" subtraction word problem; (c) a multistep word problem requiring both an addition AND a subtraction step on three fraction amounts; (d) a "same whole" validity trap — the problem describes two fraction amounts measured against DIFFERENT-sized wholes (e.g., 1/2 of a small pizza vs. 1/2 of a large pizza) that cannot be validly added or compared, reinforcing the same-whole constraint from 4.NF.2 in a new operational context.
- **Requires intro (CFU gate):** Yes — translating story language into "this needs addition" vs. "this needs subtraction" is a real skill distinct from the computation itself, and the same-whole trap (bucket d) is a genuine validity question most students have never been asked before; CFU should include a question asking the student to identify why a same-whole-violating setup CAN'T be solved by simply adding the numerators, not just compute a valid case.
- **Memorization fact (periodic re-check candidate):** No.

### Unit 4: Multiplying a Fraction by a Whole Number
- **Goal:** See a fraction a/b as "a copies of 1/b," and multiply a fraction by a whole number, including in word problems.
- **Standard:** 4.NF.4a/4b/4c
- **Buckets:** (a) repeated-addition framing with a UNIT fraction (e.g., 5 × 1/3 = 5/3), tying directly back to Unit 1's decomposition idea; (b) multiplying a NON-unit fraction by a whole number (e.g., 3 × 2/5), where the product may be improper and must be converted to a mixed number; (c) a word problem requiring the student to set up "whole number × fraction" from a real scenario themselves (e.g., "each lap is 3/4 mile; how far after 5 laps?") rather than being handed the expression; (d) a case where the product comes out to a WHOLE number exactly (e.g., 4 × 3/4 = 3) — an edge case that catches students who assume a fraction-times-whole-number answer must always look "fraction-y."
- **Requires intro (CFU gate):** Yes — this is a brand-new operation; CFU should explicitly connect it back to the a/b-as-multiple-of-1/b idea (e.g., "why is 3 × 2/5 the same as 6 × 1/5?") rather than presenting multiplication-by-a-fraction as an isolated new rule.
- **Memorization fact (periodic re-check candidate):** No.

### Unit 5 (Capstone): Fraction Line Plots
- **Goal:** Build a line plot from a small set of measurements given in fractions of a unit, then use the plot's data to answer addition and subtraction questions.
- **Standard:** 4.MD.4
- **Buckets:** (a) read a given, already-built line plot and answer a direct "how many measurements show X" question; (b) use the plot to compute a TOTAL (sum) of several data points — an addition question grounded in real data rather than an abstract expression; (c) use the plot to compute a DIFFERENCE between two data points or groups of points — a subtraction question grounded in the plot; (d) build a line plot from a short raw list of measurements (placing dots correctly above fractional tick marks) — the "make a line plot" half of the standard, not just reading one someone else made.
- **Requires intro (CFU gate):** Yes — the line plot is a new representation (a number line of dots at fractional positions) layered directly on top of the add/subtract skill from Units 2-3; CFU should walk through converting a cluster of dots at one tick into a sum before asking the student to do it unassisted.
- **Memorization fact (periodic re-check candidate):** No.

## Open questions / flags for the builder
- **Denominator mismatch for Unit 5:** 4.MD.4's own standard text restricts line-plot data specifically to halves, fourths, and eighths (1/2, 1/4, 1/8) — a narrower, power-of-2-only set, unlike Units 1-4's general 2-10 denominator range (kept consistent with `IntroToFractionsLab`). Confirm Unit 5's generator intentionally uses only {2, 4, 8} as denominators rather than inheriting the rest of the lab's wider range — using the wider range would be off-standard for this specific sub-skill.
- Unit 1 (decompose) and Unit 2 (add/subtract) are sequenced back-to-back since decomposition is the conceptual basis for addition itself (per the standard's own 3a→3b→3c progression) — confirm this isn't better merged into one unit if the builder finds the content too thin split apart.
- This lab assumes `IntroToFractionsLab` is unconditionally a prerequisite with no gating/check of that fact at runtime (no cross-lab prerequisite enforcement exists elsewhere in this codebase, as far as this planning pass found) — flagging in case the builder wants to add a soft "you may want to finish Intro to Fractions first" note on this lab's Home card rather than a hard lock.
