# FractionsFoundationsLab

**Proposed slot:** MG6 or MG7 — provisional, confirm at build time per project convention.
**Grade:** 3
**Standards covered:** 3.NF.1 (understand 1/b as the quantity formed by partitioning a whole into b equal parts, and a/b as a parts of size 1/b), 3.NF.2a-b (understand a fraction as a point on a number line; represent 1/b and a/b on a number line diagram), 3.NF.3a-d (equivalence by reasoning about size/same point on the number line; recognize/generate simple equivalent fractions; express whole numbers as fractions and recognize fractions equivalent to whole numbers; compare two fractions with the same numerator OR the same denominator, record with >,=,<, valid only for the same whole).
**Relationship to existing labs:** This lab is the direct PREREQUISITE to the already-built `MG9_IntroToFractionsLab` (grade 4 slot), whose units (Fraction Forms, Equivalent Fractions, Comparing Fractions, Simplifying Fractions) assume a student already understands what a fraction IS before tackling forms/equivalence/comparing in more depth. This lab's job is building that basic concept: a fraction as a count of equal-size parts and as a point on a number line. Scope is deliberately limited to 3.NF's narrower denominator set (2, 3, 4, 6, 8) and does NOT duplicate MG9's more advanced equivalence-generation or multi-denominator comparing work — this lab builds the basic concept and simplest same-numerator/same-denominator comparisons only; MG9 extends into different-denominator comparison, simplifying, and mixed/improper forms.
**Audience framing:** Built for a student seeing this content for the first time, regardless of the standard's posted grade level. "Fractions are numbers" is arguably the single hardest new idea in grade 3 — students arrive thinking of fractions only as "parts of a pizza," not as points that sit on the same number line as whole numbers. Every unit must ground the idea in a visual model (fraction bars/circles for the parts-of-a-whole idea, a number line for the points-as-numbers idea) before any bare symbolic a/b notation or comparison practice, with explicit CFU checks for understanding at each conceptual leap.

## Units

### Unit 1: What a Fraction Is (Unit Fractions and a/b)
- **Goal:** You will be able to look at a shape split into equal parts and name the fraction for one part (like 1/4) or several parts (like 3/4), and explain what the top and bottom numbers each mean.
- **Standard:** 3.NF.1
- **Buckets:**
  - Identify/name the unit fraction 1/b from a picture showing a whole split into b equal parts with one part shaded, across denominators 2, 3, 4, 6, 8
  - Identify/name a/b (a>1) from a picture with a parts shaded, across the same denominator set
  - Build/shade a picture to match a given fraction (the reverse, production direction — given "3/8," shade 3 of 8 equal parts)
  - Non-example / trap bucket: a shape split into UNEQUAL parts with some shaded, where the student must recognize the picture does NOT represent a valid fraction the way it's drawn (equal-size parts is the defining requirement, not just "some parts shaded")
- **Requires intro (CFU gate):** Yes — first exposure to fraction notation itself; common misconception is treating the denominator as "the number of shaded parts" and the numerator as something else, or accepting an unequal-parts picture as valid because the shaded COUNT matches without checking the parts are equal size; CFU must directly test the vocabulary (what does the bottom number mean vs. the top number) and the equal-parts requirement.
- **Memorization fact (periodic re-check candidate):** No — this is a concept a student should always be able to re-derive by looking at a picture; nothing here is a fixed fact to recall independent of reasoning.

### Unit 2: Fractions on a Number Line
- **Goal:** You will be able to find and place a fraction like 1/4 or 3/4 at its correct point on a number line between 0 and 1 (or beyond).
- **Standard:** 3.NF.2a-b
- **Buckets:**
  - Place/identify a unit fraction 1/b on a number line already partitioned into b equal intervals between 0 and 1
  - Place/identify a non-unit fraction a/b on the same kind of pre-partitioned number line
  - Partition an UNMARKED number line into b equal intervals first, then place a given fraction — the harder, two-step version where the student must create the equal spacing themselves rather than just reading pre-drawn tick marks
  - Fractions greater than 1 on a number line extending past the first whole (e.g. placing 5/4 or 6/4, requiring the student to see the line continuing past "1" with the same-size unit intervals)
- **Requires intro (CFU gate):** Yes — this is the conceptual leap from "fraction = part of a shape" to "fraction = a point/number," which many students have never seen before; CFU should include a question asking directly why a fraction can be a point on a number line the same way whole numbers are, not just a request to place one.
- **Memorization fact (periodic re-check candidate):** No — placing fractions on a number line is a spatial/reasoning skill re-derivable each time from the partitioning idea, not a memorized fact.

### Unit 3: Fractions Equal to Whole Numbers
- **Goal:** You will be able to recognize when a fraction is actually the same amount as a whole number (like 4/4 = 1, or 6/3 = 2), and write whole numbers as fractions.
- **Standard:** 3.NF.3c
- **Buckets:**
  - Recognize a/b = 1 when a = b, across the denominator set (e.g. 2/2, 4/4, 6/6, 8/8) using a picture showing the whole fully shaded
  - Recognize a/b equal to a whole number greater than 1 (e.g. 6/3 = 2, 8/4 = 2) using a picture or number-line point landing exactly on a whole-number tick
  - Express a given whole number AS a fraction with a specified denominator (e.g. "write 3 as a fraction with denominator 4" → 12/4) — the reverse, production direction
  - Non-example / trap bucket: a fraction close to but NOT equal to a whole number (e.g. 5/4 is not 1, 7/8 is not 1) paired with the true cases above, so students can't pattern-match "numerator and denominator are close" as "equals a whole number"
- **Requires intro (CFU gate):** Yes — recognizing a/b as a disguised whole number is a genuine "aha" that doesn't follow automatically from Units 1-2; common misconception is assuming any fraction with numerator ≥ denominator automatically equals a whole number (it doesn't, unless numerator is an exact multiple of denominator) — the trap bucket directly targets this.
- **Memorization fact (periodic re-check candidate):** No — this is reasoning about the relationship between numerator/denominator, always re-derivable; not a fixed fact.

### Unit 4: Equivalent Fractions (Same Point, Different Name)
- **Goal:** You will be able to tell when two fractions name the same point on the number line (like 1/2 and 2/4), using a picture or number line to see why.
- **Standard:** 3.NF.3a-b
- **Buckets:**
  - Recognize visual equivalence: two differently-partitioned pictures (or number-line points) of the same whole showing the same shaded amount, limited to simple pairs within the 2/3/4/6/8 denominator set (e.g. 1/2 = 2/4, 1/2 = 3/6, 1/3 = 2/6, 1/4 = 2/8)
  - Generate ONE simple equivalent fraction for a given simple fraction using a picture (e.g. given 1/3 shown, find its equivalent with denominator 6) — kept to this narrow denominator set, not general n×a/n×b multiplication reasoning (that extension is MG9's job)
  - Explain WHY two fractions are equivalent using the number-line "same point" idea (not a rule to apply blindly, a reasoning check)
  - Non-equivalent near-miss pairs (e.g. 1/3 vs. 2/4) presented alongside true equivalent pairs, so the skill being tested is genuine size-reasoning, not "these both look like simple fractions so they must match"
- **Requires intro (CFU gate):** Yes — this is the direct setup for MG9's deeper equivalent-fractions unit; CFU should confirm the student understands equivalence as "same point/same size," not as a numerator/denominator manipulation trick, since that symbolic generalization (n×a/n×b) is explicitly deferred to MG9.
- **Memorization fact (periodic re-check candidate):** No — equivalence reasoning here is tied to a visual/number-line model each time, always re-derivable; this lab deliberately stops short of teaching a memorizable cross-multiplication-style shortcut (that's for later).

### Unit 5: Comparing Fractions (Same Numerator or Same Denominator)
- **Goal:** You will be able to compare two fractions and tell which is greater, as long as they share the same top number or the same bottom number, and explain why using a picture.
- **Standard:** 3.NF.3d
- **Buckets:**
  - Same denominator, compare numerators (e.g. 3/8 vs. 5/8 — more shaded parts of the same-size whole means greater)
  - Same numerator, compare denominators (e.g. 1/3 vs. 1/6 — the genuinely counterintuitive case where the LARGER denominator means a SMALLER fraction, since the whole is cut into more, smaller pieces)
  - Same-whole requirement as an explicit trap: two pictures of DIFFERENT-size wholes (e.g. a big circle cut into 4 vs. a small circle cut into 2) where the fractions aren't comparable the way they would be for the same whole — testing that students know the comparison is only valid when the wholes match
  - Equal case (e.g. 4/8 vs. 4/8, or a same-numerator/same-denominator pair) requiring the "=" symbol rather than defaulting to > or <
- **Requires intro (CFU gate):** Yes — the same-numerator case (more pieces = smaller pieces = smaller fraction) directly contradicts the "bigger number = bigger value" intuition students bring from whole numbers, and is explicitly the standard's named misconception; CFU must target this directly, plus the same-whole requirement, which is easy to skip past silently.
- **Memorization fact (periodic re-check candidate):** No — comparison here is reasoning from a visual model each time (which denominator means bigger/smaller pieces), always re-derivable; not a fixed fact.
