# 3rd grade

## What they can read
- The last year of phonics standards (RF.3.3): prefix and suffix meanings,
  common Latin suffixes, multisyllable words, irregular words. Fluency
  (RF.3.4) is the big goal.
- Text band for 2nd–3rd: about 420–820 Lexile. End of year about F&P level P.
- This is the year before "reading to learn." Many students can read a word
  problem alone, but there's a wide spread: some read like 5th graders, and
  a large group still decodes slowly. **A 3rd-grade word problem should be
  readable by a strong 2nd-grade reader.** Read-aloud stays available.

## What they understand by ear
- A short paragraph with a question; a two-step story told in order.

## Limits
| | Working limit |
|---|---|
| Sentence | 15 words or fewer |
| Word problem | up to 4 sentences, question last |
| Steps in one problem | up to 2 (3.OA.8) |
| New math words per screen | 1 |
| Extra (unneeded) numbers in a story | none until the skill is solid, then on purpose |

## Grammar: yes / not yet
- **Yes:** comparative and superlative forms (*more, most, longer, longest*)
  (L.3.1g); regular and irregular verbs and simple tenses (L.3.1d–e);
  *because, when, so, if* joining clauses (L.3.1h); compound and complex
  sentences (L.3.1i). A short "If…" sentence is fine.
- **Not yet / keep rare:** passive voice ("24 apples are shared by 4
  friends"); relative clauses with *who, which, that* (L.4.1a);
  *whom/whose*; perfect tenses (L.5); noun phrases more than 4 words long;
  questions built around a negative ("Which is NOT…").

## Math words
- **Known from earlier grades:** sum, difference, digit, place value, even,
  odd, array, rows, columns, inch, foot, centimeter, meter, halves, thirds,
  fourths, a.m., p.m.
- **New in 3rd:** multiply, multiplication, times, product, factor; divide,
  division, quotient; equal groups; unknown; equation; a letter standing for
  the unknown (3.OA.8); round, nearest ten / hundred (3.NBT.2); fraction,
  unit fraction, numerator, denominator, equivalent, whole, on the number
  line (3.NF); area, square unit, perimeter, side length (3.MD); mass, gram,
  kilogram, liquid volume, liter; minute and elapsed time (3.MD.1); scaled
  picture and bar graphs; rhombus, quadrilateral.
- **Formal property names are not required.** CCSS 3.OA.5 says students
  apply the properties of operations but need not use their names. Don't
  ask for "commutative / associative / distributive" as vocabulary.
- **Not yet:** remainder (4th, 4.NBT.6), multiple, prime, "times as many"
  (4th), decimal, mixed number (4th), expression, parentheses (5th), *per*
  (6th).

## Word problems
- **Addition and subtraction:** all types, within 1,000, including two-step.
- **Multiplication and division (CCSS Table 2, 3rd):** equal groups, arrays,
  area, with:
  - unknown product ("4 bags, 6 apples in each. How many apples?")
  - group size unknown ("24 apples in 4 bags, the same number in each. How
    many in each bag?")
  - number of groups unknown ("24 apples, 6 in each bag. How many bags?")
- **Not yet:** multiplicative comparison ("3 times as many") — that's 4th
  (4.OA.1–2).
- **Two-step (3.OA.8):** tell the story in time order and put each step in
  its own sentence. The question goes last and asks for one thing.

## Watch for
- **"Times"** also means occasions ("3 times a week"). That sentence in a
  multiplication lab is a trap.
- **"Product"** also means something a store sells. **"Factor"** isn't an
  everyday word for kids.
- **"Each / every / in all / per."** *Each* is the 3rd-grade word for group
  size. Save *per* for 6th.
- **"Divided by" vs. "divided into."** "12 divided by 3" vs. "12 divided
  into 3 groups" vs. "3 divided into 12" (which reverses the order). Use
  *divided by* or "share equally among."
- **"Round"** also means circle-shaped. **"Area"** also means a place. **"Volume"**
  also means how loud.
- **Fraction words.** "One fourth" vs. "a fourth" vs. "one quarter"; *of* is
  fine for "1/4 of the shape," but "1/2 of 8" (fraction times whole number)
  is 4th–5th.
- **Elapsed time** stories pack in several time words (*begins, ends, later,
  until, after*). Keep it to one start time and one change.

## Rewrites
| Before | After |
|---|---|
| If the total number of apples is distributed equally among 4 baskets, how many apples will be placed in each basket? | There are 24 apples. Put the same number in each of 4 baskets. How many apples go in each basket? |
| The perimeter of a rectangle whose length is 8 cm and whose width is 3 cm is ___. | A rectangle is 8 cm long and 3 cm wide. What is its perimeter? |
| Sam reads 3 times a week. He reads 4 pages each time. *(in a multiplication lab)* | Sam reads on 3 days. He reads 4 pages each day. How many pages does he read? |
| Which property is shown? 4 × 5 = 5 × 4 | Is 4 × 5 the same as 5 × 4? Draw the arrays to check. |
