# 2nd grade

## What they can read
- Most decode one-syllable words reliably. This year (RF.2.3): long vowels
  in two-syllable words, vowel teams, common prefixes and suffixes, words
  with irregular spelling.
- Text band for 2nd–3rd: about 420–820 Lexile (CCSS Appendix A). End of year
  about F&P level M.
- Reading is still slow for many, and below-grade readers are common. A
  student can read a short prompt, but a three-sentence story uses up most of
  their working memory. **Keep read-aloud on.**

## What they understand by ear
- Stories of 3–4 sentences, with a question at the end.
- Compound sentences ("Sam had 12, and he gave 5 away").

## Limits
| | Working limit |
|---|---|
| Sentence | 12 words or fewer |
| Word problem | 2–3 sentences, question last |
| New math words per screen | 1 |
| Feedback | 1 short sentence |

## Grammar: yes / not yet
- **Yes:** irregular plurals *feet, children, teeth, mice* (L.2.1b — note
  "feet," which 2.MD needs); irregular past tense *gave, took, ate, ran,
  found* (L.2.1d); compound sentences; adjectives and adverbs.
- **Not yet:** passive voice, *if…then* inside a word problem, relative
  clauses, long noun phrases ("the total number of red and blue marbles in
  the bag").

## Math words
- **Known from earlier grades:** tens, ones, digit, equal, greater than / less than,
  >, =, <, halves, fourths, o'clock.
- **New in 2nd:** hundreds, place value, expanded form; even, odd
  (2.OA.3); array, rows, columns (2.OA.4); sum, difference; estimate;
  inch, foot, yard, centimeter, meter, measure, length; line plot, picture
  graph, bar graph; dollar, cents, quarter, dime, nickel, penny (2.MD.8);
  a.m., p.m.; quadrilateral, pentagon, hexagon, cube, angle (counting
  angles), face; thirds; equal shares.
- **Not yet:** multiply, product, factor, divide, quotient, fraction
  notation such as 1/3 (3rd), round (3rd), area, perimeter.

## Word problems (CCSS Table 1, 2nd), within 100
- **All the addition and subtraction types, now to mastery,** including the
  two hardest (met in 1st, expected to be solid by the end of 2nd):
  - **Start unknown:** "Some birds were on a tree. 5 flew away. Now 8 are
    left. How many were there at first?"
  - **Compare problems where the words point the wrong way:** "Lucy has 3
    fewer apples than Julie. Lucy has 5. How many does Julie have?" ("fewer"
    in the words, but the answer comes from adding). These are the hardest
    word problems in K–2. Introduce them with the two-rows picture.
- **One- and two-step** problems (2.OA.1). Two-step is new: make the two
  steps visible (two questions, or a picture with two parts) before asking
  them in one story.

## Watch for
- **"Difference"** (everyday: "what's different?") vs. math (how far apart).
- **"Even"** means three things: *even* numbers, "now it's even" (equal), and
  "even though."
- **"Odd"** also means strange.
- **"Foot / feet"** is a body part and an irregular plural. **"Yard"** is
  also a backyard.
- **"Quarter"** now also means the coin (25¢). Show it.
- **Homophones the voice can't tell apart:** *sum/some, cents/sense,
  whole/hole.* Make sure the sentence and picture show which one is meant.
- **"In all," "altogether," "left"** are idioms; teach them, and don't use
  them as keywords that tell the student which operation to pick.

## Rewrites
| Before | After |
|---|---|
| Julie has 3 more apples than Lucy, who has 5 apples. How many apples does Julie have? *(relative clause)* | Lucy has 5 apples. Julie has 3 more than Lucy. How many apples does Julie have? |
| Estimate the length of the pencil in inches. | About how many inches long is the pencil? |
| What is the sum of 36 and 25? *(when "sum" is new)* | 36 + 25 = ? *Then later:* "Find the sum. Sum means the answer when you add." |
