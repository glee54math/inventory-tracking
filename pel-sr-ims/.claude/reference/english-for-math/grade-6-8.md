# Grades 6–8

## What they can read
- Text band for 6th–8th: about 925–1185 Lexile.
- Decoding is done for most. The load now is **academic language**: abstract
  nouns, Greek and Latin roots (L.6.4b), and dense sentences. Students with
  reading gaps (and English learners) are still in every class; a word
  problem in plain English is fair to everyone.
- Read-aloud isn't built in at this level here; text must stand on its own.

## Limits
| | Working limit |
|---|---|
| Sentence | 25 words or fewer |
| Word problem | a short paragraph; one question at the end |
| New math words per screen | 2, each with an example |

## Grammar
- Everything is fair to *read*, but for *math questions*: prefer active
  voice, one condition per sentence, and the question last. Long noun
  phrases ("the ratio of the number of boys to the total number of students
  in the class") are the biggest single cause of misreading at this level.
  Break them up.

## Math words
- **6th:** ratio, rate, unit rate, *per*, *for every*, *for each*, percent
  (6.RP); quotient of fractions; integer, negative, positive, opposite,
  absolute value, rational number (6.NS); variable, expression, term,
  coefficient, factor, equation, inequality, solution, dependent /
  independent variable (6.EE); statistical question, distribution, mean,
  median, interquartile range, mean absolute deviation (6.SP); net, surface
  area.
- **7th:** proportional relationship, constant of proportionality; markup,
  markdown, discount, tax, tip, commission, simple interest, percent error
  (7.RP.3); additive inverse; probability, sample, population, random,
  inference; scale drawing; circumference; supplementary, complementary,
  vertical, adjacent angles.
- **8th:** square root, cube root, irrational, scientific notation; function,
  input, output, slope, rate of change, y-intercept, linear, nonlinear;
  system of equations; translation, rotation, reflection, dilation,
  congruent, similar; Pythagorean theorem, hypotenuse; scatter plot,
  association.

## Translating words into math
- **"Less than" reverses the order:** "5 less than x" is x − 5, not 5 − x.
  Same for "subtract 5 from x," and "the quotient of a and b" (a ÷ b).
- **"Is" means "=":** "Twice a number is 14."
- **Inequalities:** *at least* (≥), *at most* (≤), *no more than* (≤), *more
  than* (>). These are everyday phrases with exact meanings (6.EE.8). Teach
  each one with a number line.
- **Ratio language** has many forms: "3 to 2," "3:2," "for every 3 … there
  are 2," "3 per 2." Introduce one form at a time.
- **Percent contexts (tax, tip, discount, interest)** assume knowledge of
  money from outside school. One sentence explaining what a tip *is* helps
  a lot of students.

## Watch for
- **Spoken minus vs. negative:** "−3" should be said "negative three"; "8 − 3"
  is "8 minus 3." Text a voice or student reads must keep them apart.
- **"Mean"** (unkind), **"range"** (stove, mountains), **"rate,"
  "interest"** (curiosity), **"similar"** (everyday: kind of alike; math:
  exact same shape), **"reflection"** (thinking), **"opposite,"
  "absolute,"** **"function"** (a party, a job).
- **"Per" vs. "percent."** Sound alike, different ideas.
- **"Deeper / colder / lower"** with negative numbers: bigger in words,
  smaller as a number (−20 is colder than −5). The comparison words fight
  the order of the numbers.
- **Statistics vocabulary** (*distribution, variability, spread*) is abstract.
  Show the plot every time the word appears.

## Rewrites
| Before | After |
|---|---|
| The ratio of the number of boys to the total number of students is 2:5. If there are 30 students, how many are boys? | A class has 30 students. For every 5 students, 2 are boys. How many boys are in the class? |
| Determine the value of x for which 3 less than twice x is equal to 11. | Twice a number, minus 3, is 11. What is the number? |
| The temperature decreased by 8 degrees from −3°. | It was −3°. Then it got 8 degrees colder. What is the temperature now? |
