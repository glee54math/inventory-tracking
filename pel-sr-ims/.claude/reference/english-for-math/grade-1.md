# 1st grade

## What they can read
- Start of year: CVC words and a small set of sight words, read slowly.
- During the year (RF.1.3): digraphs (*sh, ch, th*), silent-e words (*make,
  five*), common vowel teams, two-syllable words, endings *-s, -ed, -ing*.
  End of year: simple sentences with some fluency (about F&P level I–J).
- **Reading takes effort, and that effort uses the attention the math
  needs.** Even a student who *can* read the prompt does better when it's
  spoken. Keep the voice primary.

## What they understand by ear
- Two-step directions, short stories of 2–3 sentences.
- Conjunctions *and, but, or, so, because* (L.1.1g); personal and possessive
  pronouns (*he, she, they, his, her*) (L.1.1d); prepositions (L.1.1i).

## Limits
| | Working limit |
|---|---|
| Spoken sentence | 10 words or fewer |
| Written sentence the student might read | 8 words or fewer, decodable or sight words |
| Story | 1–2 sentences + the question |
| Instructions at once | 1 action |
| Feedback | a few words |

## Grammar: yes / not yet
- **Yes:** simple past tense (*had, gave, ate* — in pictures and by ear),
  "and / but / so," a pronoun that refers to the sentence just before.
- **Not yet:** *if…then*, passive voice ("5 were eaten"), relative clauses
  ("the boy who has more"), negatives in questions ("Which is NOT…").

## Math words
- **Known from K:** count, how many, more, fewer, same, add, take away, plus,
  shapes, longer/shorter.
- **New in 1st:** tens, ones, digit (1.NBT.2 uses it: "the two digits of a two-digit number" — teach it with a base-ten picture); the equal sign means *the same as* (1.OA.7);
  the symbols > = < (1.NBT.3); *greater than, less than*; halves, fourths,
  quarters, *half of, fourth of* (1.G.3); o'clock, half-hour (1.MD.3); length
  unit; *how many more / how many fewer* when comparing data (1.MD.4);
  trapezoid, half-circle, quarter-circle, rectangular prism.
- **In the standards, but teacher words first:** *sum* (1.OA.6) and *addend*
  (1.OA.4, "unknown-addend problem"). Fine to use with students once a
  picture has taught them; until then say "the total" and "the parts."
- **Not yet:** difference, place value (as words to read), expanded form,
  even/odd, equation (as a word), commutative.

## Word problems (CCSS Table 1, 1st), within 20
- Everything from K, plus:
- Add to / Take from, **change unknown**: "3 birds. Some more came. Now
  there are 7. How many came?"
- Put together, **one part unknown**.
- Compare, **difference unknown**: "Ann has 7. Ben has 4. How many more does
  Ann have?"
- Compare, bigger/smaller unknown **where the words point the right way**:
  "Ben has 4. Ann has 3 more than Ben. How many does Ann have?"
- Three numbers that add up (1.OA.2).
**Meet, but don't require mastery:** start unknown ("Some birds were there.
3 more came. Now there are 7. How many were there at first?") and compare
problems where the words point the wrong way ("Ann has 3 more than Ben.
Ann has 7. How many does Ben have?"). CCSS Table 1 says 1st graders work
with these but need not master them until 2nd. In a 1st-grade lab, give
them the most picture support, keep their sentences the shortest, and
think twice before making them a required bucket or a Test question that
counts toward passing.

## Watch for
- **The equal sign.** Many students think "=" means "the answer goes next."
  Say "is the same as" in early items, and include 7 = 3 + 4 so the answer
  isn't always on the right.
- **"Fourth" and "quarter" mean three things.** *Fourth* in line, *one fourth*
  of a shape; *quarter* of a pizza, *quarter past*, a quarter coin (2nd).
  Show the picture whenever the word is said.
- **"More" is not a signal to add.** In compare stories "more" can mean
  subtract. Teach the picture (two rows lined up), not the keyword.
- **"Ones" vs. "one."** "4 tens and 3 ones" — say it with base-ten pictures.
- **Half past** is an idiom; show the clock.

## Rewrites
| Before | After |
|---|---|
| Mia had some stickers. She got 4 more. Now she has 9. How many did she have at first? *(start unknown, 2nd grade)* | Mia has 5 stickers. She gets 4 more. How many now? |
| Which number sentence is true? | Is this true? 7 = 3 + 4 *(spoken: "Is 7 the same as 3 plus 4?")* |
| Determine the number of tens and ones. | How many tens? How many ones? *(two questions)* |
