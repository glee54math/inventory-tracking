# 5th grade

## What they can read
- Text band for 4th–5th: about 740–1010 Lexile. End of year about F&P level V.
- Can handle a short paragraph and complex sentences. Academic vocabulary
  keeps growing fast, so new math words still need teaching, not assuming.

## Limits
| | Working limit |
|---|---|
| Sentence | 20 words or fewer |
| Word problem | up to 6 sentences |
| New math words per screen | 1–2 |

## Grammar: yes / not yet
- **Yes:** perfect tenses (*has eaten, had saved*) (L.5.1b–c); correlative
  conjunctions *either/or, neither/nor, both/and* (L.5.1e); relative clauses.
- **Keep rare:** passive voice in the question itself; a sentence with three
  or more clauses.

## Math words
- **Known from earlier grades:** times as many, multiple, factor, prime,
  remainder, decimal, tenths, hundredths, mixed number, angle words.
- **New in 5th:** expression, numerical expression, parentheses, brackets,
  braces, evaluate (5.OA.1–2); exponent, power of 10 (5.NBT.2);
  thousandths; fraction as division (5.NF.3); *of* meaning multiply (5.NF.4);
  scaling, "resizing" (5.NF.5); volume, cubic unit, unit cube, rectangular
  prism, base, height (5.MD.3–5); coordinate plane, axis, x-axis, y-axis,
  origin, ordered pair, coordinate (5.G.1); categories of shapes
  ("every square is a rectangle") (5.G.3–4); common denominator, least
  common denominator (LCD), benchmark fraction (0, 1/2, 1), and the
  mixed-number words *borrow* (also called regroup) and *carry* (5.NF.1–2).
  Pick one word for each idea, and teach it with a picture before a hint
  uses it.
- **Not yet:** variable (as a formal word, 6th), ratio, rate, per, percent,
  negative numbers, integer (6th).

## Word problems and translating words into math
- **Words become expressions (5.OA.2):** "Add 8 and 7, then multiply by 2"
  means 2 × (8 + 7). The *order of the words* and the *order of the
  operations* don't match. Students need explicit practice going both ways
  (words → expression, expression → words).
- **"Of" means multiply:** "2/3 of 12." In everyday English *of* is just a
  connecting word; here it carries the operation.
- **Fraction division stories (5.NF.7):** "How many 1/3-cup servings are in 2
  cups?" vs. "Share 1/2 a pan among 3 people." The two wordings mean
  different divisions. Use one wording at a time until both are taught.
- **"Is three times as large as" without computing it (5.OA.2)** —
  describing an expression in words is itself a skill here.

## Watch for
- **"Base"** (bottom face of a prism) vs. base ten vs. baseball.
- **"Power"** (exponent) vs. strength. **"Origin"** vs. where something came
  from. **"Plot"** (a point) vs. a story's plot.
- **"Evaluate"** vs. "value." Pick one instruction ("Find the value") and
  keep it.
- **Brackets, braces, parentheses:** three new names for marks that look
  alike. Show them side by side once.
- **Exponents read aloud:** "10 to the third power" / "10 cubed" — the voice
  must say it the way the lab teaches it, not "10 3."
- **"Every square is a rectangle"** uses *every / all / some* as logic. Check
  that the question doesn't hinge on a trick quantifier.

## Rewrites
| Before | After |
|---|---|
| Write an expression representing the quantity obtained when the sum of 8 and 7 is doubled. | Write an expression: add 8 and 7, then double it. |
| Determine the volume of the rectangular prism, given that its dimensions are 3 by 4 by 5. | A box is 3 cm long, 4 cm wide, and 5 cm tall. What is its volume? |
