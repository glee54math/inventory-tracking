# Kindergarten

## What they can read
- At the start of the year most can't read. Many know some letters and can
  spot their own name.
- By the end of the year: letter sounds, simple CVC words (*cat, sit, big*), and a
  handful of sight words (*a, the, I, see, is, you, to, and, my, go, can,
  like*) (RF.K.3). Patterned books: "I see a cat." (about F&P level C–D)
- **Assume no independent reading.** Every word a student needs must be
  spoken, and the picture must carry the problem.

## What they understand by ear
- Everyday spoken sentences, simple questions, and one- or two-step oral
  directions ("Tap the dog, then tap Check").
- Question words *who, what, where, when, why, how* (L.K.1d).
- Position words: *above, below, beside, in front of, behind, next to*
  (K.G.1 and L.K.1e prepositions).
- Plurals with -s/-es (*cat → cats, box → boxes*) (L.K.1c).

## Limits
| | Working limit |
|---|---|
| Spoken sentence | 8 words or fewer, one idea |
| Instructions at once | 1 action |
| On-screen text the student must read | none (numerals, pictures, 1–2 sight words on a button) |
| Feedback | 2–5 words ("Yes! Five!" / "Count again.") |

## Grammar: yes / not yet
- **Yes:** present tense, "How many…?", "Tap the…", "Which has more?",
  simple "and."
- **Not yet:** *if / then*, *each*, *than* sentences that are longer than
  "more than 5," passive voice, two clauses in one sentence, pronouns that
  point back to an earlier sentence.

## Math words
- **Known on arrival:** some number words by ear (often to 10), *big/little*,
  *more* (in the sense of "I want more").
- **New in K (teach with a picture):** count, number, how many, more, fewer,
  less, same, equal; add, plus, take away; circle, triangle, square,
  rectangle, hexagon, cube, cone, cylinder, sphere; flat, solid; longer,
  shorter, taller, heavier, lighter; sort; the position words above.
  (K.CC.6 says "greater than, less than, equal to." For K students, say
  *more / fewer / the same* and save "greater" for later.)
- **Not yet:** numeral, digit, symbol, sum, difference, equation (as a word to
  read), decompose, attribute, cardinality, partner, place value.

## Word problems (CCSS Table 1, K)
Told aloud and acted out with objects or pictures, within 10:
- Add to / Take from, **result unknown**: "3 birds. 2 more come. How many
  now?"
- Put together / Take apart, **total unknown** and **both parts unknown**:
  "5 apples. Some red, some green. How many of each could there be?"
Not yet: start unknown, change unknown, compare problems.

## Watch for
- **Teen numbers say the order backwards.** "Four-teen" says the 4 first;
  14 writes the 1 first. *Eleven* and *twelve* follow no pattern at all.
  This causes the "41 for fourteen" mistake. Show ten-frames every time a
  teen word is said.
- **Number words are hard to read.** *One, two, eight* aren't spelled the way
  they sound. Matching written number words to numerals is reading, not
  math: voice the word, and keep it to *one–ten* for K.
- **"Left" has two meanings.** "How many are left?" (still there) vs. "the
  left group" (side). Don't use both in one lab; prefer "How many are still
  there?" and pictures over left/right.
- **"Fewer" is rarer than "less" in children's talk.** Teach it with a
  picture before asking it.
- **"Same" vs. "equal."** Use *the same* first; bring in *equal* with the
  picture and say it the same way every time.
- Don't put the answer in the voiced story ("There are 5 cats…" in a "how
  many cats?" problem).

## Rewrites
| Before | After |
|---|---|
| Determine which group contains fewer counters. | Which group has fewer? Tap it. |
| Count the counters and write the numeral. | Count the dots. Type how many. |
| How many are left? *(in a lab that also says "left group")* | How many are still there? |
| Great job! You correctly identified that both groups are equal. | Yes! They are the same. |
