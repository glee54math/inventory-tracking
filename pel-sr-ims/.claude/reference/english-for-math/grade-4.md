# 4th grade

## What they can read
- Phonics standards are done. Now it's morphology: reading unfamiliar
  multisyllable words by their parts (RF.4.3).
- Text band for 4th–5th: about 740–1010 Lexile. End of year about F&P level S.
- **"Reading to learn" begins, and subjects suddenly use far more academic
  words.** Students who were fine in 3rd often stall here (the "4th-grade
  slump"). The math is often fine; the words are new.

## Limits
| | Working limit |
|---|---|
| Sentence | 18 words or fewer |
| Word problem | up to 5 sentences |
| Steps | multi-step (4.OA.3) — but say each fact in its own sentence |
| New math words per screen | 1–2, each defined by a picture or example |
| Extra (unneeded) information | okay on purpose, once the skill is solid |

## Grammar: yes / not yet
- **Yes:** relative pronouns *who, whose, which, that* (L.4.1a)
  ("a number that is greater than 50"); progressive tenses; modal verbs *can,
  may, must* (L.4.1c); prepositional phrases.
- **Keep rare:** passive voice; nested clauses (two "that"s in a sentence);
  perfect tenses (5th); *neither/nor* (5th).

## Math words
- **Known from earlier grades:** multiply, divide, product, quotient, factor,
  fraction, numerator, denominator, equivalent, area, perimeter, round.
- **New in 4th:** *times as many / times as much* (multiplicative comparison,
  4.OA.1–2); factor pair, multiple, prime, composite (4.OA.4); remainder
  (4.NBT.6), and interpreting it (4.OA.3); pattern, rule; "ten times" in
  place value (4.NBT.1); round to any place; mixed number; common
  denominator; decimal, tenths, hundredths, decimal point (4.NF.6); point,
  line, line segment, ray, angle, degree, protractor, right / acute / obtuse,
  perpendicular, parallel, line of symmetry (4.G, 4.MD.5–7); converting
  units (km, m, cm; kg, g; lb, oz; L, mL; hr, min, sec).
- **Not yet:** expression, parentheses / brackets (5th), exponent (5th),
  volume (5th), ratio, per, percent (6th), negative numbers (6th).

## Word problems
- **Multiplicative comparison (new, CCSS Table 2):** "A blue hat costs $6. A
  red hat costs 3 times as much. How much is the red hat?" This sits right
  next to additive comparison ("$3 more"). Students mix them up; give
  practice that sets the two side by side.
- **Multi-step with remainders (4.OA.3):** the answer depends on what the
  remainder *means* ("How many vans are needed?" rounds up; "How many full
  vans?" doesn't). The English decides the answer. Teach that directly.
- **Fractions in stories:** adding and subtracting with like denominators;
  a whole number times a fraction ("5 friends each eat 1/4 of a pizza").

## Watch for
- **"3 times as many" vs. "3 more than."** The most important language
  contrast this year.
- **"Right"** angle vs. right side vs. correct answer. **"Degree"** also
  means temperature. **"Acute"** sounds like "a cute."
- **"Multiple"** also means "many." **"Prime"** also means "best."
- **Decimal reading.** Say "four tenths," not "point four," so the words
  teach the place value (4.NF.6). The voice needs the same rule.
- **Remainder words:** *left over, extra, remaining.* Pick one per unit.
- **Unit names with prefixes:** *kilo-, centi-, milli-* are morphology this
  grade is learning. Show the meaning ("kilo means 1,000").

## Rewrites
| Before | After |
|---|---|
| A van that holds 8 students is being used to transport 30 students; how many vans are required? | 30 students are going on a trip. Each van holds 8 students. How many vans do they need? |
| The red ribbon's length is three times that of the blue ribbon, which measures 4 feet. | The blue ribbon is 4 feet long. The red ribbon is 3 times as long. How long is the red ribbon? |
| Classify the angle. | Is this angle right, acute, or obtuse? |
